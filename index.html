Getting started

There are three ways to begin. Popular trucks fills in the common ones in a single tap. Find my vehicle walks make, model and year range. I know my specs skips straight to entering numbers.

Whichever you use, the measurements stay visible and editable. The lookup is a starting point, not a guarantee — trim, drivetrain and dually axles all change hub specs. Check the numbers against your actual hub before you submit.

The eight measurements

Hub bolt pattern
Number of studs on the vehicle hub and the circle they sit on, like 6x5.5. Count the studs, then measure the circle.
Hub bore
Outside diameter of the centre hub the wheel slides over, in millimetres.
Vehicle stud thread
Thread on the studs already on your truck. This is what the adapter threads onto.
Wheel bolt pattern
Pattern of the wheel you are fitting. Usually in the wheel listing.
Wheel centre bore
Diameter of the hole in the back of the wheel, in millimetres.
Thickness
How far the adapter pushes the wheel out, measured face to face. Studs add height on top of this.
Pressed stud thread
Thread on the new studs your wheel mounts to. It does not have to match the vehicle side.
Quantity
Two per axle. Most orders are 2 or 4.

Flags and blocks

Every change you make runs against our rule set, written by the people who machine these parts. Two outcomes matter:

Block means we will not build it. Usually the geometry does not leave enough material between the through-bolts and the pressed studs. Changing the thickness will not fix a block — the specs have to change.

Flag means it is probably fine but a person needs to look first. Flagged designs go through instead of getting a price on the spot, and our team quotes them by email.

Each check shows its rule number, like R-201. Quote that number if you call us about it.

Pricing

Standard combinations price instantly, per pair, with free shipping in the USA. Anything carrying a flag shows Quote required instead — we price those by hand because they need a set of eyes on them first.

Price depends on lug count, thickness, and whether the adapter changes bolt pattern or just spaces the wheel out.

After you submit

  1. Draft — the drawing built from your numbers. Nothing sent yet.
  2. Submitted — it reaches our office for review.
  3. Under review — checked for safety, design, production cost and time.
  4. Quoted — we create an invoice and email it to you.
  5. Paid — payment creates your order number.
  6. Production — a production order goes to the floor.
  7. Quality control — checked at the QC station.
  8. Shipped — tracking number and photographs of your parts.

You get an email at every change.

Changing a design

You have 12 hours from submitting to change a design yourself. Open My designs and the countdown is on the row. Inside that window, Edit loads it back into the designer.

After 12 hours, or once we have quoted it, changes need re-approval and may add cost — the longer a job sits, the longer it takes to fulfil. Use Request a change and our team will pick it up.

An unsent draft has no time limit. It stays in your list until you submit or delete it.

For our team

The Admin tab holds the rule set and the pricing formula. Rules can be switched off, retuned, or moved between Block and Flag without a developer.

Changes stack up as unsaved edits and go live the moment you press Publish, which increments the version and writes to the change log. The catalogue check beside the pricing form shows live whether the formula still reproduces our published prices — if a number turns red, stop.

Two people should review any rule change before it is published. These parts hold wheels onto trucks.

Terms

Bolt circle diameter
The circle the studs sit on. The 5.5 in 6x5.5 is this, in inches.
Hub centric
The wheel is carried by the centre bore rather than the studs. Preferred, and quieter at speed.
Lug centric
The wheel is centred by the lug nuts alone. Works, but needs careful torquing.
Spacer vs adapter
A spacer keeps the same bolt pattern and only pushes the wheel out. An adapter changes the pattern as well.
6061-T6 billet
The aluminium we cut these from. Solid extruded stock, not cast.
